How the 3D Tab Works

The 3D tab appears when a 3D mode is loaded. It controls the whole 3D stage: camera, objects, lights, backdrop, floor, finishing effects and the impact note. Every group in it needs Pro, Elite and Agency. Anyone can pick a 3D mode, watch it and export it. Modes still in development show their settings locked until the mode ships.

At the top is Quality, then the mode's own look, then six groups: Camera & Motion, Objects, Lighting & Shadows, Scene & Platform, Effects & Grade and Audio. Groups and sections only show when the current mode uses them.

Every 3D mode starts with its own tuned look. The first row names it; its info icon explains why the mode looks that way. Change any control and that section becomes yours while the rest keeps the mode's look. Reset look (shown once you have changed something) brings the whole look back. Switching modes never overwrites your own settings.

Quality

One click sets how crisp shadows, reflections, contact shading and edges are. Your export uses this too. It never turns features on or off, and never changes how many objects a mode places.

LevelWhat it sets
LowFastest: low shadow and mirror resolution, fastest contact shading, no antialiasing, standard preview sharpness.
MediumBalanced for most machines.
HighCrisp shadows and reflections.
UltraSharpest, and heaviest on the graphics card.
CustomWhere you land after hand-tuning a quality control. Keeps exactly what was on screen.

Technical parameters

  • Camera Moves appears only in modes that make a move of their own on top of the shared camera, such as following a falling chain or framing a whole board. Its switches and sliders are specific to that mode and explained by their info icons.

Tip

  • Set Seconds To Peak close to your clip length so the peak lands at the end.

Tip

  • A slightly warm key with a cool fill or sky colour stops a render looking flat and grey.
  • For a crisper shadow, lower Focus Range before raising Shadow Quality.
